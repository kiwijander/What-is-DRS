Formula 1 explained

What is DRS in F1? Explained in two minutes

DRS stands for Drag Reduction System. It is a button that lets a driver open a flap on the rear wing to go faster on a straight so it is easier to pass the car in front.

See how it works Read the rules

How DRS works

Three steps on every lap with a DRS zone.

1. Detection

A timing line on the track just before the DRS zone, it measures the gap between a car and the car ahead.

2. Activation

If the gap is one second or less the driver gets a signal. Once in the zone the driver presses a button on the steering wheel.

3. Flap opens

The upper flap of the rear wing lifts. Less air pushes back on the car so the top speed goes up. Braking closes the flap again.

DRS rules

DRS is not a free speed boost. It only works when these conditions are met during a race.

  • WhereOnly inside marked DRS zones, usually on the longest straights.
  • GapOnly for a car within one second of the car ahead at the detection point.
  • TimingNot allowed during the first two laps of a race or after a restart.
  • WeatherRace control can switch it off for example when the track is wet.

DRS has been retired in 2026

DRS was used from 2011 to 2025. For the 2026 season Formula 1 brought in new cars with active aerodynamics. The front and rear wings can move, switching between a low-drag setting on straights and a high-downforce setting in corners. A new overtake mode gives a chasing car extra electrical power when it is close behind another car.

Read more at Motor Sport Magazine or PlanetF1.

DRS questions

A car following closely loses grip because the car ahead disturbs the air often called dirty air. That made passing hard. DRS gave the chasing driver a speed advantage on the straight to make overtakes more likely.

Not during a race. Only the chasing car within one second can open the flap. In qualifying and practices drivers can use the DRS in the zones without needing another car nearby.

Opinions were split. Some fans felt it made passing too easy while others said it was needed to get cars past each other at all. Both views were common over the 15 years it was used.
